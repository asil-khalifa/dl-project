The Indian Space Research Organisation, or ISRO, is the national space agency of India. It was founded on 15 August 1969 under the leadership of Vikram Sarabhai, who is regarded as the father of the Indian space programme. ISRO is headquartered in Bengaluru and works under the Department of Space, which reports directly to the Prime Minister.

India's first satellite, Aryabhata, was built by ISRO and launched on 19 April 1975 by a Soviet rocket. The first launch vehicle developed in India, the SLV-3, placed the Rohini satellite in orbit in July 1980, making India the sixth nation with its own satellite launch capability. The Polar Satellite Launch Vehicle, or PSLV, first flew in 1993 and has become ISRO's most reliable rocket. In February 2017 a single PSLV mission carried a record 104 satellites into orbit.

ISRO's first mission beyond Earth orbit was Chandrayaan-1, launched on 22 October 2008. Its Moon Impact Probe helped confirm the presence of water molecules on the lunar surface. The Mars Orbiter Mission, also called Mangalyaan, was launched on 5 November 2013 and entered orbit around Mars on 24 September 2014, making India the first country to reach Mars on its first attempt. The mission cost about 450 crore rupees, far less than comparable missions by other agencies.

On 23 August 2023, Chandrayaan-3 made India the first nation to land a spacecraft near the south pole of the Moon. The lander was named Vikram and the rover Pragyan. A few days later, on 2 September 2023, ISRO launched Aditya-L1, its first mission to study the Sun from the first Lagrange point. ISRO's heavy-lift rocket, the LVM3, will carry the Gaganyaan capsule, which is intended to take Indian astronauts to low Earth orbit.

ISRO also serves everyday needs. The INSAT and GSAT satellites provide television, telephone and weather services, while the NavIC constellation gives India its own regional satellite navigation system. Remote-sensing satellites of the IRS series support agriculture, forestry and disaster management across the country.
